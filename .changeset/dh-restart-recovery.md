Recover DH issuance after a process restart from stores alone. `Dhke.approve` now lets a new exchange from a registry's issuer overwrite a held root that was never used: the registry has no update accepted or escrowed. The old root is overwritten only when the new exchange retains its own, so an interruption before that leaves the old root; once an update is staged or accepted the refusal is unchanged. `Manager.putRegistryRoot` takes an optional `overwrite` flag for this and `Manager.hasRegistryRoot` reads root presence without decryption. A process now refuses a second live DH operation on the same registry. The runtime issuance owner settles records an earlier process left active on its first `processOnce`: an issuance that had not anchored reads `interrupted` and needs a new Grant, a holder that kept its root waits and completes by itself when the update is accepted (`Dhke.resume`, `Dhke.finish` proves the held root against the update, a wrong root is refused with a reason and the Grant stays unaccepted), and an issuer that anchored but did not finish completes without signing or anchoring again. Nothing is signed after a restart. A Grant past its deadline reads `expired`, with a reason that asks for a new Grant when the update had already arrived. A KERIpy peer keeps the old rule and refuses a second exchange on a registry it holds a root for, so with a KERIpy holder the issuer needs a fresh registry.
