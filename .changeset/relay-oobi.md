An edge runtime resolves OOBIs and schemas through its relay and never fetches a third party's URL. For a job whose origin is not the relay's, the Oobiery asks the relay to resolve it with the existing `/oobis` exchange, sent through Poster and signed by a local identifier that has authorized the relay as its mailbox, then fetches the result from the relay's own OOBI endpoint and verifies it as it does for a direct fetch. A controller, witness, agent or mailbox OOBI `.../oobi/{cid}/{role}[/{eid}]` is read from `oobi/{cid}/{role}[/{eid}]` on the relay, and a schema OOBI `.../oobi/{said}` from `oobi/{said}`. While the relay answers 404, is unreachable, or answers without the controller's KEL, the job stays queued in state `relay-requested` (or `relay-pending` before the relay can be asked) and jobs waiting on the relay take turns. After `RELAY_OOBI_TIMEOUT_MS` (30 seconds from the first relay attempt, on the runtime clock) it fails as `relay-unresolved` or `relay-unserved`. Another non-OK answer fails at once, as before, and a URL naming neither an identifier nor a SAID fails as `relay-unmappable-oobi`. The relay's own OOBI and any URL on an accepted relay location are fetched directly. The Oobiery takes an `OobiRelaying` (relay, Poster and clock), or null, after its cues, and `isRelayOrigin` exposes the relay-origin test the egress guard uses.

On a relaying agent (`AgentRuntimeOptions.relaying`), the `/oobis` handler accepts a request only from a served sender, by the same rule as `/fwd` relaying (`servingHost`, now exported from the forward handler). In `@keri-ts/hosting`, a relaying host's OOBI endpoint also answers for any identifier whose accepted KEL it holds, through its service habitat, as KERIpy's `OOBIEnd` does with a default habitat; `AgentRuntime.relaying` tells the host it relays. A runtime without `relay` or `relaying` behaves as before.
